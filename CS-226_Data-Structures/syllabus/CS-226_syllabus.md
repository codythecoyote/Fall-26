Course Description
This course covers the design and implementation of fundamental data structures, including arrays, stacks, queues, linked lists, binary trees, heaps, balanced trees, hash tables, and graphs. Other topics include basic algorithms (sorting, balancing search trees, graph search algorithms), asymptotic analysis, and programming in Java (object-oriented programming, generics, unit testing).

Prerequisites [official]: A grade of C+ or better in EN.500.112 OR EN.601.220 OR AP Computer Science OR EN.500.132. Students cannot register until grades for prerequisites are posted.

Prerequisites [in plain English]: Object-oriented programming in Java (or C++)

Course Learning Outcomes
Upon successfully completing this course, you should be able to:

Explain, trace, analyze, and implement the operation of common data structures and algorithms.
Use analysis techniques to choose the data structure/implementation appropriate for a given problem.
Write advanced object-oriented solutions in Java to significant problems by implementing appropriate data structures and algorithms.
Evaluate and compare the time complexity of functions using mathematical techniques.
Design an algorithm that produces the correct results according to specified inputs and time or space complexity constraints.
This course will address the following Computer Science BS Program Student Outcomes:

Analyze a complex computing problem and apply computing principles and other relevant disciplines to identify solutions. [SO1]
Design, implement and evaluate a computing-based solution to meet a given set of computing requirements in the context of the program’s discipline. [SO2]
Apply computer science theory and software development fundamentals to produce computing-based solutions. [SO6]

Assessment and Grading
This course is assessed through periodic tests, a final exam, and class participation.

Each of the three tests is worth 20% of your course grade, and the final exam is worth 38%. The final exam is comprehensive. See the Exams page for the exam format and schedule.

The remaining 2% of your course grade comes from class participation: attending lecture and working through in-class worksheet activities. There are 40 scheduled lectures this semester. You earn the full 2% by attending 35 of them (about 12 weeks’ worth). The remaining 5 absences are built into the policy so you do not need to explain or document why you missed a lecture — illness, travel, interviews, anything — so please do not email asking for an excused absence.

Your course grade is the sum of your three test scores (20% each), your final exam score (38%), and your participation score (2%). Letter grades for the course are subject to the instructor’s evaluation of your overall class performance, generally based on the standard scale: 90s → A, 80s → B, 70s → C, 60s → D, below 60 → F; S/U follows the same ranges, with C and above counting as Satisfactory. The cutoff for assigning -/+ to each grade letter is further subject to the instructor’s evaluation of your overall class performance.

Homework
The coursebook chapters include worked examples, review, and practice problems for each topic. You are expected to work through these as you go. They are not collected or graded, but they are the best way to prepare for the exams.

Two additional homework assignments are also assigned during the semester, graded with feedback but not counted toward your course grade. See the Homework page for what these are and why homework grades do not count.

You may discuss coursebook problems and homework with classmates (see the Academic Integrity policy below).
